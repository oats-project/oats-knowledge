# What the probe measured

A converted copy of the accepted base (418 concepts), queried by subject from independent bare blobless clones over
local `file://` transport, three cold and warm trials per workload. Reading one committed subject index fetched one
blob; scanning every concept's frontmatter fetched all 418. Cold medians: the index about 0.1–0.2 s; a scan with an
explicit missing-blob prefetch about 0.28–0.54 s (2.1–2.8 times the index); an unprefetched lazy scan about 10.4 s
(one blob fetch per concept). Warm queries fetched no blobs and their times overlapped, so the index is not a
universal warm-latency win. The unchanged 5.8 validator accepted the converted bundle, but it accepts any non-empty
`type` and ignores unknown keys, so a green result is format conformance, not ontology conformance.

# What follows

The case for committed views is selective I/O, predictable blob demand and a ready agent-readable view, not that
scans are unusable. Views are generated by the package, committed in the base and read as files; the scan runs at
regeneration (the proposer's `propose --check` and the maintainer before merge), and the subject layout needs its
own schema checks and a regenerate-and-diff gate (`okf-reindex.mjs --check`) because the 5.8 validator cannot
certify it. The cost is recorded in
[readers tolerate a stale index; writers refuse it](/nodes/oats-okf-expert/decisions/readers-tolerate-a-stale-index-and-a-missing-id-writers-refuse-them.md):
the view format is frozen and a renderer change costs every bridged base a regeneration PR.

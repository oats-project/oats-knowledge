# The wrong question

"Specialist or generalist?" assumes the expertise sits in the instance. In
OATS it sits in the soul: its text, its skills and the area's node with its
decisions and lessons
([durable specialization](/nodes/oats-maintainer/decisions/durable-specialization.md)).
A fresh instance of the right expert soul therefore has a clean context
window and the area's knowledge at once, and a generalist soul is itself the
expert of the cross-area node. The question a lead actually faces is
"whose knowledge fits this unit of work, and where does a hand-off earn its
cost?"

The source reached this after first framing the trial as specialists
against generalists and being corrected by the human: the point of souls is
fresh experts that benefit from their area's knowledge. Anchoring and a
crowded window are properties of long-lived instances, not of experts.

# What the trials showed, once the framing is right

Both arms of the T7 v2 paired trial ran the same model and started from
the same knowledge content; one read it as a clone, the other bound it
through nodes. The difference was how work was shaped around it.

- **Coding went to the whole-unit session.** The generalist arm passed more
  oracle checks in well under half the slot time and never hit the cap. The
  expert arm's coding deficit came from the machinery in its merge path
  (the dispatcher hop, the developer split, the label hand-back to a gate),
  not from having experts; see
  [sorting a trial's costs](/nodes/oats-expert/lessons/sort-a-trials-costs-into-design-instructions-or-measurement.md).
- **Ownership and context went to the expert arm.** Its findings became
  recommendations and were fixed
  ([out-of-lane findings](/nodes/oats-expert/lessons/out-of-lane-findings-go-to-the-owner-as-a-recommendation.md)).
  Its design read the accepted decisions it was changing, the release plan
  and who co-decides what, noticed an existing rule that contradicted CI,
  and recommended an answer to nearly every open question. The generalist's
  design was good and twice as fast, but left its questions without
  recommendations, did not know who had asked for it, and needed
  re-approval when a bot found gaps after the humans had signed off.
- **Every generalist miss was outside its task.** That is the class of
  defect that grows with hidden coupling and with months of drift, which
  neither trial was large or long enough to show.
- **On small work one session matched teams** in the earlier T6 trial, at
  a fraction of the cost, with correctness separating the arms only on the
  one out-of-scope trap.

# The rule the lead applies

1. **Keep the unit of work whole**, in one fresh session of the soul whose
   area it mostly lives in, which builds what fits its window and merges on
   agreement under the workspace's review protocol. This is the lead's existing
   [integrated-plan direction](/nodes/oats-expert/decisions/lead-expert-authors-the-cross-area-plan.md)
   applied to execution, not a change to it.
2. **Add a hand-off only where ownership or context needs one:** a fresh
   reviewer's check at a head, an area owner's decision, a finding routed
   to whoever will act. Each hand-off costs a wake and a fresh read of a
   node; the trials showed it pays for itself where the alternative is an
   unowned finding or an unread decision, and not as a station in the
   merge path.
3. **Keep standing instances only for what must persist between events:**
   the front door humans talk to
   ([front-door direction](/nodes/oats-maintainer/decisions/front-door-routes-and-introduces.md)),
   and open commitments, which can equally live on the forge with a
   scheduled wake that spawns a fresh owner. This shapes an effort's own
   seats; standing lane maintainers and the rest of the
   [roster](/nodes/oats-maintainer/decisions/domain-expert-rebuild.md) are
   the maintainer's decisions and are not changed here.
4. **Curate the node, because a fresh expert knows only what was written
   down.** The value of the fresh-expert shape rests on the area's node
   being current and consulted; the tree stays the truth.

The source reports T7 v2 (2026-11-06 to 09, sixteen paired tasks in scratch
deployments: generalist arm 142 of 156 oracle checks in about 24 hours of
slot time, redesign arm 126 of 156 in about 41 hours, the generalist faster
on thirteen tasks) and T6 (2026-11-03/04, 25 counted runs on two models,
n=3 per cell: every arm accepted all efforts; one session cost about $1-2 a
run against $9-16 for the redesign and $27-29 for today's roles). The
reading of the two design tasks is the source's own; the blind judge never
ran. The trial did not cleanly test a fresh expert per effort: the
dispatcher gave several efforts to one live expert. The expert arm's design
advantage may partly come from its review by three area experts, which
fresh per-area reviewers could supply. T6's case for teams on work that
exceeds one window or spans people is untested there. T7 v2 was stopped at
slot 32 of 34 and its records deleted at the human's request, so the
figures survive only in the named notes; no transcript, kit or forge record
was inspected and no trial was repeated.

The human's hypothesis that opened this reading, and any roster direction
given since, are not claimed here: the hypothesis is
source-reported, and direction decisions belong to the maintainer's
[coordination redesign](/nodes/oats-maintainer/stewardship/coordination-redesign-precedes-adaptive-onboarding.md),
whose owner records acceptance. This lesson is the lead's design heuristic
for shaping an effort, not an adopted roster, a changed role or a new
authority.

# A child follows the brief's letter

A lead sometimes holds material whose owner said it must stay off anything
public: a partner organisation's proposal, a private schema, internals the
partner described in confidence. When the lead passes it to a child for a
design or a comparison, the child does exactly what the brief says. In the
source's effort the brief said "read in place, no text into the public
design". The child copied no text. It described the document's contents,
attributed them ("their proposal says") and derived facts from them (a
private schema's size), in a design on a branch of a public package
repository. Its harvest's backing notes carried two more of the partner's
details towards the knowledge base, which is public too.

Each of those is disclosure, though none is copying. The owner's rule was
"keep it off anything public", which covers paraphrase, attribution and
derived facts; the brief had narrowed it to text.

The lead caught it at verification, about ten minutes after the commit,
had the child scrub the text and rewrite the branch, and asked the running
harvester to leave those details out.

# Rule: the brief names the destinations and the owner's rule as given

Before passing private material to a child:

- check the visibility of every place the child's work can land: the
  repository its branch is pushed to, the knowledge base its harvest feeds,
  any document it writes or comments on. Name each one in the brief, public
  or private. A child does not know which of its outputs are public unless
  the brief says so;
- brief the owner's rule as the owner put it ("nothing from it, described
  or copied, goes anywhere public"), not a narrower rule of the lead's own
  ("no text"). The owner's wording is the one the lead can answer for;
- say where any reasoning that needs the material goes instead: a private
  file in the child's home, relayed to the lead by message, never into the
  public artefact;
- cover the child's harvest. Its notes are proposed at a checkpoint and
  published to the base by a harvester the lead does not steer, so the
  brief tells the child that notes about this material stay out of its
  proposal or state the lesson without the partner;
- at verification, search the head for the owner's name and the material's
  distinctive terms before reading anything else. A disclosure found ten
  minutes after the commit is still fetchable by its old commit id until
  the forge purges it; one found before the push is not.

# Elimination route

A brief template with a field for "inputs that must not reach a public
destination, and every destination this work has" would make the omission
visible at the source. A pre-push search of the head for the material's
owner and distinctive terms, as the deployment-specifics lesson proposes
for host and human names, catches the remainder before the forge keeps
it. Neither exists yet; this is the lead's step at briefing and
verification, and this harvest changes no skill.

# What happened

A lead wrote a set of source-of-truth documents for a merge of two products,
from its person's decisions and several seats' reviews. In one working day,
three statements it had written as the person's decisions turned out to be its
own:

- From the person's rule "an invitation that leaves a scope of trust is
  approved by a person" the lead derived "every spawn onto another machine
  needs a person's approval" and wrote it as the rule. It contradicted a
  decision the other side holds (no person's login in spawn, wake or retire)
  and would have stopped scheduled remote spawns that run today. The other
  side's maintainer caught it.
- The lead listed three renames as owner decisions. They had come from
  reviewers' reads. A reviewer asked for the source, and there was none.
- Told "approve when a machine is connected, drop the extra conditions", the
  lead relayed to another seat "and nothing afterwards: no per-operation
  approval". The person corrected it within the hour: a configuration chosen
  by each team will say which operations need a person.

Each derivation was plausible. Each was treated as the person's word by the
next reader until someone asked where it came from.

# The lesson

- **Every statement in a plan carries its source:** the person, with the date;
  a named seat; or "my derivation". A derivation is never written under the
  person's label, however directly it seems to follow.
- **A derivation is checked before it is written as a rule,** against two
  things: the recorded decisions of every side it touches, and what runs today
  that it would stop.
- **A person's short answer is relayed in the person's words.** What it
  implies beyond them goes back to the person as a question, not on to
  another agent as a fact.
- **A request to confirm a derivation says that it is one,** and what it would
  change.

# Why it matters

A source-of-truth document is read by seats that cannot ask the person. They
review, design and build against what it says the person decided. A derivation
under the wrong label is corrected only when a reader happens to hold the
decision it contradicts.

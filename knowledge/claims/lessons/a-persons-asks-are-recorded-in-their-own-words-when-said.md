# Lesson

In a long effort with one person, he said he had asked for four things: research on a writing style,
ids for automations, how onboarding should work, "and there were more". None was in the lead's log,
the knowledge base or any other instance's notes. The lead had kept decisions and rulings, not asks
that were not acted on at once. Two of the four were recovered a day later, only because he said them
again with more detail. One is still without its detail.

Separately, the lead published a list titled as coming from his comments and filled it with its own
findings. He saw at once that his asks were not in it.

**The rule.**

- Write every ask of the person in his own words, with the time, when it is said: in the log, and in
  a living note of asks if the effort is long. An ask that is not acted on now is the one that gets
  lost.
- A list presented as his holds his asks first, in his words. The lead's own findings go under their
  own heading.
- When he says he told you something and no record holds it: say so plainly, search every record,
  ask his maintainer and dispatcher, then ask him once. Never reconstruct an ask from what seems
  likely.
- When he asks for a list of what a system needs, write what is needed and not how to build it. He
  corrected a first draft that listed mechanisms.
